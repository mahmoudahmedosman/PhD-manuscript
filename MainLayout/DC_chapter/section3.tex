\section{pre-DC1}

As part of my PhD work, I led the analysis of pre-DC1, a modified version of the \textsc{Lemaître} DC1 in which the supernova light curves 
are generated using a SALT model that satisfies the NaCl constraints, by using NaCl itself. This ensures that the simulations are self-consistent with the model 
assumptions used in the analysis, allowing us to isolate potential biases introduced by the pipeline itself, rather than those arising 
from a mismatch between the simulation and the fitted model. Using these self-consistent pre-DC1 simulations, we can assess the accuracy 
of the reconstructed parameters and their uncertainties on \textsc{Lemaître}-like datasets.

\subsection{Simulation Step}

All of the elements concerning the generation of the pre-DC1 supernovae can be summarized in the following list: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item All of the supernova data were generated with NaCl using the actual observation logs of ZTF, SNLS and HSC.
    \item The cosmology that was used is Planck18 found in astropy.cosmology ($\Omega_m=0.30966$) (\cite{Planck_2018_params}).
    \item The color and stretch of the supernovae were drawn from a normal distribution centered around 0, with $\sigma(c) = 0.1$ and $\sigma(X_1) = 1$.
    \item There are no redshift errors added to any supernovae.
    \item A spectrum is generated for each supernova from ZTF and SNLS drawn from a phase when the supernova was observed.
    \item Magnitude cuts were applied before the application of any noise and intrinsic dispersion. For each survey the magnitude cuts are written in table \ref{tab:mag_cuts}. This ensures that there are no selection effects added to the simulations.
    \item The rate of the supernovae was chosen to be Frohmaier+19 (\cite{Frohmaier_2019}).
    \item The Milky Way dust extinction was drawn from the Planck dust map and the $E(B-V)_{MW}$ values follow the CCM89 dust law (\cite{Cardelli_1989}).
    \item The addition of the intrinsic dispersion, $\sigma_{int}$, is applied on the observed magnitudes of each supernova.
    \item Light curve measurement uncertainties were drawn from sncosmo \footnote{\url{https://sncosmo.readthedocs.io/en/stable/}} as a function of the skynoise taken from the real observational logs.
    \item An error snake is also added to the light curves drawn from a normal distribution with a dispersion of 5\% of the flux. The error snake term is the same for a given band and given night for a given supernova.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Goals}

The goal of the pre-DC1 analysis is to assess the consistency of the 
Georges pipeline (see Section \ref{sec:georges}) in reconstructing the input supernova distances and 
cosmological parameters. To this end, the complete analysis chain is applied 
to 100 realizations of the pre-DC1 dataset, which allows us to assess both the 
accuracy of the reconstructed parameters and the validity of their estimated 
uncertainties. 

More specifically, we first verify that the reconstructed supernova 
parameters $X_0$, $X_1$, $c$, and $t_{max}$ are unbiased with respect 
to their input values. The reliability of the parameter uncertainties is 
tested by comparing the uncertainties obtained from the inverse Fisher 
matrix described in Equation \ref{eqn:nacl_cov}, $\sigma_{\text{Fisher}}$, with those derived from the empirical 
covariance matrix measured across the 100 realizations, 
$\sigma_{\text{Emp}}$. We then assess whether the reconstructed distance 
moduli $\mu$ are unbiased. Finally, we test whether the cosmological parameters 
$\Omega_m$ and $w$ are unbiased when the analysis is performed assuming a 
flat $w$CDM cosmology with a constant equation-of-state parameter $w$. 


\section{Results: Potential Biases \& Systematics}
For the pre-DC1 simulations, we deliberately reduce the effective ZTF supernova sample to approximately 500 supernovae per simulation, corresponding to a 
total sample of $\sim 1500$ supernovae across the simulations. 
The average time it takes to train one pre-DC1 sample of supernovae is $\sim$ 1h30mins on the IN2P3 Computing Cluster\footnote{\url{https://cc.in2p3.fr/en/}}. 
The duration of each step in the training recipe can be summarized in Table \ref{tab:pre_dc_log}\\

    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{ l | l }
    \hline
        Step & Time \\
        \hline
        Light curve fitting & $\sim$30 seconds\\
        \hline
        Spectrum recalibration & $\sim$30 seconds\\
        \hline
        Initial model guess & $\sim$1 hour\\
        \hline
        Initial error model guess & $\sim$1 minute\\
        \hline

We therefore expect a full 
\textsc{Lemaître}-like sample to take of order 4 hours to process, which is longer 
than initially anticipated but still perfectly manageable.

We present the model and data agreement results on a randomly selected 
realization of pre-DC1.

\subsubsection{Photometry}

Figure \ref{fig:dc1_lc_pulls} shows the photometric weighted 
residuals normalized by the measurement uncertainties as a function 
of phase. The weighted residuals are centred around zero across the full phase 
range covered by the model, indicating that NaCl describes the 
photometric data without systematic phase-dependent biases. 
Figure \ref{fig:dc1_lc_band_resid} shows the pull distributions 
per band. Each distribution is fitted with a Gaussian that has a standard deviation of 1. 
The fitted means are 
consistent with zero, confirming that the model does not 
introduce band-dependent biases.

To assess whether the description of the data is accurate accross different redshifts, Figure \ref{fig:dc1_lc_chi2} shows 
the photometric squared weighted residuals evaluated per supernova as a function of 
redshift. 
No redshift-dependent trend is observed, demonstrating 
that NaCl provides a uniform description across the full redshift 
range of the sample. Finally, Figure \ref{fig:dc1_lc_resid} shows 
the local photometric squared weighted residuals, stacked in phase-wavelength space. 
The description remains accurate throughout the model's domain of 
definition.

In conclusion, we have shown that our re-implementation of NaCl is able to 
reproduce the generated data extremely well after fitting. No wavelength-, 
phase-, or redshift-dependent bias is visible in the residuals. We now turn to 
the reconstruction of the supernova parameters of interest, ($X_0$, $X_1$, $c$), which 
constitute the basic ingredients for estimating supernova distances.

\subsection{Parameter Reconstruction and Covariance Matrix}

\subsubsection{Parameter Reconstruction}

Since the pre-DC1 dataset is generated with a model that respects NaCl constraints, we expect to reconstruct the exact same parameters used in the generation. 
In Figure \ref{fig:sn_pars_wres} we compare the generated and reconstructed $X_0$, $X_1$, $c$ and $t_{max}$ parameters averaged over all 100 realizations of pre-DC1. 
This is done by calculating the weighted residuals for each parameter. This is observed as a function of redshift 
to verify whether NaCl introduces redshift-dependent biases on the supernova parameters. 
We see that the reconstructed parameters match the input parameters within the statistical uncertainties of the reconstruction.\\


In Figure \ref{fig:sn_pars_wres}, we compare the generated and reconstructed values of the $X_0$, 
$X_1$, $c$, and $t_{max}$ parameters for one realization of the pre-DC1 sample. The 
structure of the sample is clearly visible, with ZTF populating the low-redshift 
regime and SNLS and HSC extending to higher redshifts. 
For each parameter, the figure shows the normalized residual,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\text{gen}}}{\sigma_\theta}
\end{equation}
as a function of redshift. Here, the reconstructed uncertainties are obtained 
from the covariance matrix, approximated by the inverse Fisher matrix. 

Three main conclusions can be drawn. First, no significant bias is observed in 
the parameter reconstruction. Second, there is no evidence for a 
redshift-dependent bias. Third, the normalized residual distributions have 
widths close to unity, indicating that the reconstructed uncertainties provide 
an accurate description of the actual parameter dispersion.

\subsubsection{Covariance Matrix}


As stated above, it is essential for NaCl to provide an accurate estimate of the 
training covariance matrix, in particular for the supernova parameters of interest, 
($X_0$, $X_1$, $c$), including the off-diagonal terms. One of the purposes of the data 
challenges is therefore to process a sufficiently large number of realizations 
to construct an empirical covariance matrix and compare it with the covariance 
matrix inferred from the fit Fisher matrix. \\

We first consider the scale of the predicted and empirical variances, i.e. the 
diagonal terms of the covariance matrix. In Figure \ref{fig:pars_cov}, we compare the 
Fisher-derived uncertainties, $\sigma_{\text{Fisher}}$, obtained from a single pre-DC1 
realization with the empirical uncertainties, $\sigma_{\text{Emp}}$, estimated from the ensemble 
of 100 pre-DC1 realizations, as a function of redshift. The lower panel shows 
that the inverse Fisher matrix provides a good prediction of the empirical 
uncertainties. We nevertheless observe that the Fisher-derived variances are 
systematically smaller than the empirical ones by about 5\%, with little 
dependence on redshift. As discussed below, this small discrepancy is most 
likely due to the limited number of realizations used to estimate the empirical 
covariance matrix. 

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    %\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{MainLayout/DC_chapter/figures/band_resid.pdf}
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{MainLayout/DC_chapter/figures/new_fig/coverage_all.png}
    \caption{\textit{Top}: $\sigma_{\text{Fisher}}$ in red cross and $\sigma_{\text{Emp}}$ blue dots as a function of redshift. We see distinct layers in the uncertainties that are related 
    to the different parameters $X_0$, $X_1$, $c$ and $t_{max}$. \textit{Bottom}: Ratio of the $\sigma_{\text{Emp}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{Fisher}}$.}
    \label{fig:pars_cov}
\end{figure}

To assess whether 100 realizations are sufficient to estimate the 
empirical covariance, we compared covariance matrices constructed 
from 100 and 900 realizations of a previous pre-DC1 iteration. This iteration contained less supernovae and did not go through the entire \textsc{Lemaître} pipeline. 
The ratio $\sigma_{\text{Emp}}/\sigma_{\text{Fisher}}$ converges 
toward unity with increasing sample size in that previous iteration, which confirms that the 
slight overestimate of $\sigma_{\text{Emp}}$ seen in Figure 
\ref{fig:pars_cov} is a finite-sample effect rather than a 
systematic bias in the NaCl uncertainty propagation.

Finally, we investigate whether the full covariance matrix, including its 
off-diagonal terms, is also correctly reconstructed. In Figure \ref{fig:pars_corr}, we compare 
the correlation matrices derived from the Fisher covariance matrix and from the 
empirical covariance matrix. To reduce the statistical noise affecting the 
empirical estimate, the supernovae are first ordered by increasing redshift and 
the matrices are then rebinned by grouping the supernovae in bins of ten. The broad 
correlation structure observed in the empirical covariance matrix is clearly 
reproduced by the Fisher-derived covariance matrix.

\subsection{Distance Modulus and Cosmological Parameters Reconstruction}


Our re-implementation of NaCl has been integrated into the "georges" pipeline 
(Section \ref{sec:pipeline}), which chains together (1) the sample selection, (2) NaCl, (3) 
EDRIS, the distance-inference framework, and (4) \texttt{cosmologix} (\cite{Betoule_2026}), which infers cosmological parameters from the EDRIS distances. We now 
investigate whether this full analysis chain is able to recover the input 
cosmology of the simulations. It is important to emphasize that we are not 
testing the ability of EDRIS itself to recover unbiased distances, as this was 
already established in D. Kuhn's PhD thesis\footnote{\url{https://theses.hal.science/tel-05451069}}, where EDRIS was shown not to bias 
supernova distances in our analysis setup. \\

The Lemaître pipeline constrains $\Omega_{bc} = \Omega_b + \Omega_c$, the 
combined baryon and cold-dark-matter density parameter. The neutrino mass is 
fixed to the minimal value adopted in the Planck 2018 analysis. Since the 
corresponding neutrino density, $\Omega_\nu$, represents only a small fraction 
of the total matter density, we use the approximation $\Omega_m \simeq 
\Omega_{bc}$. \\

Figure \ref{fig:pre_dc1_hubble_tot} shows the mean binned distance residuals over 100 pre-DC1 
realizations, together with a comparison between the empirical uncertainties on 
the binned distances and the Fisher uncertainties predicted by EDRIS. 

We find that, although NaCl has been shown to reconstruct the supernova parameters and 
their covariance matrices satisfactorily, significant biases are observed in the 
mean distance residuals, reaching up to approximately $9\sigma$ around $z \simeq 
0.1$ and $z \simeq 1$. These redshift ranges correspond respectively to the 
transition between the ZTF and SNLS samples and to the high-redshift end of the 
SNLS/HSC sample.

The corresponding \texttt{cosmologix} results are shown in Figure \ref{fig:dc1_contour}, where we 
present the $\Omega_m$-$w$ confidence contours for six randomly selected 
pre-DC1 realizations. Some realizations recover the input cosmological 
parameters within $1\sigma$, whereas others show substantial deviations. The 
distribution of the recovered $\Omega_m$ and $w$ values over all 100 
realizations is shown in Figure \ref{fig:w_om_dist}. 

Consistent with the biases observed in the reconstructed distances, we find 
large biases in the recovered cosmological parameters, across the full set of 
100 realizations. The mean recovered values of $\Omega_m$ and $w$ are displaced 
from their input values by $15.5\sigma$ and $10.4\sigma$, respectively. The bias 
remains when fixing $w=-1$ and fitting only for $\Omega_m$, for which the mean 
recovered value is displaced from the input value by $19.4\sigma$, as shown in 
Figure \ref{fig:om_dist}. The origin of this effect is currently unknown, but it is likely 
connected to the biases already observed in the reconstructed distance moduli, 
since these distances are the direct inputs to the cosmological fit. \\

The origin of these biases has not yet been conclusively identified. One 
possibility is that the model regularization is insufficient, allowing localized 
artifacts to develop in the reconstructed model and subsequently propagate into 
the inferred distances. Another possibility is that the analysis cuts induce an 
effective selection effect. Although no explicit selection function was included 
in the simulations, the cuts preferentially remove some supernovae and may 
therefore alter the properties of the selected sample in a way that contributes 
to the observed biases. Finally, it is possible that the Fisher matrix does not 
fully capture the detailed structure of the true covariance matrix, in 
particular its off-diagonal structure. Investigations on these assumptions are 
on-going within the team in charge of the integration of the georges pipeline.

\section{Conclusion}


This chapter presented an overview of the re-implementation of the NaCl 
framework, an effort to which I made a major contribution, its first 
application to real data using the SNfactory dataset, and the validation of the 
framework on \textsc{Lemaître}-like datasets through the pre-DC1 programme, which I led. 

The results demonstrate that NaCl accurately describes the 
spectrophotometric evolution of Type Ia supernovae and that its Fisher-based 
uncertainty estimates are consistent with the empirical uncertainties for the 
large majority of parameters. The small residual underestimate of the empirical 
uncertainties is compatible with the finite number of realizations used to 
construct the empirical covariance matrix. 

However, following the integration of NaCl into the georges pipeline, 
significant biases were identified in the reconstructed distance moduli near 
the survey transition redshifts, around $z \simeq 0.1$ and $z \simeq 1$. These 
biases appear to propagate into the cosmological inference, leading to biased 
estimates of both $\Omega_m$ and $w$. 

The origin of these biases has not yet been conclusively identified. Several 
hypotheses are currently being investigated, including residual model artifacts 
associated with insufficient regularization, effective selection effects 
introduced by the quality cuts, and possible limitations in the covariance 
propagation. Resolving these biases is the main objective before the pipeline 
can be applied to the blinded \textsc{Lemaître} dataset. Accurate distance measurements 
are also important for the $f\sigma_8$ analysis, making this validation work 
directly relevant to both the dark-energy and growth-rate science goals of the 
Lemaître project.
