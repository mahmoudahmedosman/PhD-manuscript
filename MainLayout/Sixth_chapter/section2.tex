\section{Applications on self-consistent mocks}
Before running BORG-PV on the ZTF-Uchuu mocks, we need to ensure that if we run it on simulations that are generated with the exact same model as the one used in BORG-PV, 
it does not introduce systematic biases in the reconstructed density and velocity fields as well as in the parameters. \\

\subsection{Forward model analysis: profile likelihood tests}
We conduct an initial test on the forward model where we realize likelihood profiles for each model parameter and cosmological parameters. We do that by evaluating 
the forward model on the initial conditions $\delta^{IC}$ used to generate the supernovae while varying each parameters and evaluating the likelihood. We 
expect to see a peak in the likelihood profiles of each parameter at the value used to generate the mock. We show in Table \ref{tab:borg_self_table} the configuration 
used to generate the self-consistent simulations. \\

input values within 1$\sigma$.

To assess the statistical robustness of the forward model, we generate 100 self-consistent mock realisations of the supernova catalogue, each drawn from 
a different set of initial conditions while keeping the model parameters fixed at their fiducial values. For each realization, we evaluate the likelihood 
profile for every model and cosmological parameter, and compute the mean and standard deviation of the profile peaks across the 87 realizations. 
We expect that the averaged parameters coincide with the fiducial input values within the expected standard deviation. Any systematic offset would indicate a 
bias in the forward model that must be understood and corrected before applying the pipeline to real data. We plot the results in 
Figures \ref{fig:cosmo_coverage}, \ref{fig:params1_coverage}, \ref{fig:params2_coverage} and \ref{fig:params3_coverage}. We notice that the all of the parameters are well 
recovered. 
The likelihood profile of $\sigma_v$ also exhibits an unphysical peak near 0 $\text{km}.\text{s}^{-1}$. 
This feature is a numerical artifact that occurs as the denominator of the likelihood approaches zero. 
It can be avoided by imposing a more restrictive lower bound on the prior of $\sigma_v$.

\subsection{Results of the field level inference on self-consistent simulations}

We present the results of applying BORG-PV on self-consistent simulations. In Figures \ref{fig:dens_self_1}, \ref{fig:dens_self_2}, \ref{fig:dens_self_3}, \ref{fig:dens_self_4}, \ref{fig:dens_self_5} 
we show on the left the generated density field and on the right the reconstructed density field. 
We notice that the reconstructed field manages to capture some of the underlying structures visible in the true density field. We can compare 
the overdense and underdense regions within the white-dashed circle where the supernovae have been generated. 
The largest structures are recovered while small scales structures seem to be missed. 
This is expected due to the resolution of our grid ($10 \, \text{Mpc}.h^{-1}$).\\

A more quantitative result is shown in Figure \ref{fig:Pk_self} where we plot the power spectrum of the 
final density field $\delta^{F}$ calculated in the largest box within the region where the supernovae are generated and the posterior mean density field. 
We notice that the reconstructed mean density field power spectrum is in good 
agreement with the one from the generated mock within the region where the supernovae exist. The plot on the left represents the cross correlation factor $r$ defined as: 
\begin{equation}
    r(k) = \frac{P_{\text{cross}(k)}}{\sqrt{ P_{\text{true}} P_{\text{recon}} }}
\end{equation} 
where $P_{\text{cross}}(k)$ is the cross power spectrum. This metric indicates how correlated the positions of the overdensities are, where $r(k) = 1$ 
represents a perfect reconstruction. We observe a significant correlation at large scales that decreases toward smaller scales. This is expected, as the 
number of supernovae used to constrain the density field is small compared to 
the number of initial condition voxels. The $64^3$ grid amounts to $\sim 260,000$ free parameters sampled from only $\sim 1000$ supernovae. 
Since the supernovae are approximately uniformly distributed within the survey volume, their mean separation is of order $\sim 50 \, \text{Mpc}/h$, meaning 
that only modes on scales larger than this separation are properly constrained by the data. At smaller scales the reconstructed field loses correlation with the truth. \\

We can also look at the reconstructed model and cosmological parameters in 
Figure \ref{fig:param_self}. We notice that $\mu_A$, $\alpha$ and $\Omega_m$ are recovered within $1\sigma$ of their true values, while $\sigma_v$, the 
bulk flow $\mathbf{V}$ and $\sigma_8$ appear biased for this realisation. However, the number of MCMC steps is insufficient for the chain to have fully 
converged, and it is possible that running the sampler for longer would bring the parameters closer to their true values. Furthermore, we present results 
for a single simulation only. A coverage test over many realisations, as performed for the likelihood profiles, would be required to draw conclusive 
results, but was beyond the scope of the time available during this PhD. (Convergence by cutting chain in half still not done.)
