\section{General Conclusion}

Improving the constraints on the growth rate of large-scale structures 
($f\sigma_8$) is one of the central challenges of modern cosmology, as it 
provides a direct test of gravity on cosmological scales. During this PhD, 
I addressed this challenge through two complementary fronts using Type Ia 
supernovae from the ZTF survey: improving the precision of supernova 
distance measurements, and applying a field-level inference pipeline 
to reconstruct the velocity field of the nearby Universe. \\


The first front focused on improving the distance measurements of supernovae through the continued development and applications of the 
NaCl framework: an algorithm designed to train empirical Type Ia supernovae spectrophotometric models while fitting their light curves. 
The trained model uses 
the SALT2 parameterization consisting of two global surfaces ($M_0(p, \lambda)$ and $M_1(p, \lambda)$) and a color law $CL(\lambda)$. The framework normally 
requires both photometric and spectroscopic data in order to train the model. For spectrophotometric data, such as SNfactory, this required the development of 
additional conditions in NaCl in order to be able to use the data for the model training. This consisted in removing the spectrum recalibration polynomial, reintroducing 
the color law in the spectral model as well as fitting the $X_0$ parameter for each supernova directly from their spectra. 
This was then tested on the SNfactory dataset where the NaCl proved capable of training a model on real data as well as to highlight the limitations of the SALT2 parameterization 
in capturing the supernova variability in the UV, Si II and Ca II regions. 
My work also involved the development of an adaptive regularization in the model that would adapt the model in the regions where there is a lack of data. This regularization 
is not currently implemented in NaCl due to the fact that in its current state, it does not allow the minimization to fully converge. 
Further work is required to stabilize the minimization procedure, potentially through a reformulation of the regularization.

NaCl is an essential a part of the \textsc{Lemaître} project, combining data from ZTF, SNLS and HSC surveys in order to construct a Hubble with $\sim$ 3000 independent Type Ia supernovae 
within the redshift range $0<z<1.5$, the largest independent sample yet. With the development of the new Georges cosmological pipeline embedded within \textsc{Lemaître}, 
additional validation tests on simulated datasets needed to be conducted on the pipeline: the DCs. The validation of NaCl within Georges was done with DC1 and pre-DC1. The 
results show that NaCl manages to accurately describe the photometric evolution of Type Ia supernovae but still introduces biases in the cosmological results. Moreover, the 
uncertainties obtained from the Fisher matrix of NaCl have been shown to be well estimated with the exception of the uncertainties obtained on two sets of parameters $X_1$ and 
$c$. \\


The second front focused on applying the BORG Bayesian field-level inference pipeline on ZTF Type Ia supernovae. The goal of this is to obtain the most accurate reconstruction 
of the velocity field from the supernovae by directly inferring the initial conditions of the underlying matter field, and with the use of a gravity model, obtain the 
large scale structures. BORG has proven to be more accurate than other widely used methods in reconstructing the velocity field. With the development of a novel BORG-PV pipeline 
that includes an improved selection modeling and supernova prior, tests on self-consistent simulations showed the capability of accurate reconstruction of the density and velocity 
fields. The pipeline also proved capable in inferring cosmological parameters such as $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$ under the assumption of $\Lambda$CDM. 
When applied to more realistic ZTF-like simulations generated from the 
Uchuu N-body simulation, the pipeline successfully reconstructed the 
density and velocity fields, though recovering the cosmological parameters 
proved more challenging. 
These results motivate further investigation of the simulation framework and supernova modeling before applying the pipeline to real ZTF data.
These results pave the way for future analyses for the measurement of $f\sigma_8$ and for cross-survey analyses. 



\section{Future Prospects}

The work conducted during this PhD presents the base of a larger and more complete analysis of the data. On the side of NaCl, 
additional testing on capturing calibration uncertainties is needed within DC3 and a color scatter implementation is needed for DC4 before finalizing the NaCl 
framework.  
Moreover, a more complex 
error model needs to be implemented in order to capture uncertainties that are wavelength-phase-dependent as well as to spot outliers that may have 
contaminated the training dataset. 
Once that is complete, DC5 can be tested and the pipeline can operate on the full-blinded \textsc{Lemaître} dataset 
in order to obtain 
an independent measurement of the dark energy equation of state $w$ 
and its potential time variation $w_a$, with a dataset of $\sim 3000$ 
Type Ia supernovae spanning $0 < z < 1.5$. 
Running the full pipeline will also give us access to accurate measurement of the supernovae distances which will then allow us to proceed to $f\sigma_8$ measurements. \\


On the BORG-PV side, more realistic ZTF simulations are needed in order to validate the pipeline. This includes applying masks to only cover the ZTF footprint 
as well as masking the Milky Way on the line of sight. 
An important aspect not yet accounted for is the correlation between 
supernova distance measurements, as seen in Chapter \ref{chap4}. 
Accounting for these correlations within the BORG-PV likelihood is a challenge that must be met before operating on the ZTF data. 
Lastly, in order to operate on the full ZTF dataset, a more complex selection function is needed within the likelihood. 

Another aspect that can be explored within BORG-PV is the possibility of conducting a cross-survey analysis. 
One approach would be to use the Manticore-Local constrained realization 
of the local Universe as the density field for BORG-PV. 
This would allow a joint inference 
combining the velocity information from ZTF supernovae with the density 
field information from the galaxy survey, effectively performing a 
multi-tracer field-level inference. 
The exact incorporation of a multi-probe analysis is yet to be determined, and extensive testing on simulations is required before moving on to applications on real data. \\

The methods developed during this PhD lay the groundwork for future 
analyses with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST, which will provide 
an order of magnitude more Type Ia supernovae at low redshift, enabling 
tighter constraints on $f\sigma_8$ and a rigorous test of $\Lambda$CDM.
